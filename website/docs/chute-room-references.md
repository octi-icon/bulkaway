# Upper-floor chute room illustration references

Researched September 11, 2026. Scope: visual architecture for the Bulk Away chute-room service illustration.

## What the room is

The illustration needs an enclosed resident intake room on an apartment floor. NYCHA describes a small room containing the chute door on each floor and separately describes the compactor room at the bottom of the building. Its discussion also distinguishes older doors accessed directly from a corridor. This supports a clear sequence of corridor, ordinary entry door, enclosed room, then small intake hatch. [NYCHA, Garbage Chutes](https://designguidelines.nycha.info/garbage-chutes/).

## Architectural plan reference

Lowney Architecture's Creekside Hotel and Residences drawings, dated February 17, 2025, sheet B42, contain an enlarged plan titled “LEVEL 2 - 8 RESIDENTIAL B TRASH CHUTE ROOM.” The indexed drawing description identifies a rectangular 17-foot-6-inch by 8-foot-8-inch room, three chute openings along the left wall, and a hinged entry door at the upper right. The same sheet separately depicts the ground-floor discharge/pickup room with dumpsters. Use the upper-floor drawing as the spatial reference; the dimensions are project-specific, and this example has three chutes. The full PDF exceeded the browser's fetch limit, so these details were recovered from its indexed drawing content. [Lowney Architecture, Creekside Hotel and Residences, sheet B42](https://www.cityofpaloalto.org/files/assets/public/v/1/planning-amp-development-services/new-development-projects/3400-el-camino/c3_3400-el-camino_plan.pdf).

An additional small-room reference is Kaller Architecture's Pinnacle at Cypress plan set, sheet SP-5, “TYPICAL - TRASH CHUTE ROOM.” The indexed drawing distinguishes a human entry door and surrounding partition from the chute itself. This is a more compact single-chute arrangement. [Kaller Architecture, Pinnacle at Cypress, sheet SP-5](https://fortlauderdale.legistar.com/View.ashx?G=F11871F6-88F5-40B5-BD94-B6711694F9A7&GUID=6FD3DB20-3F9E-41F0-B2A9-0F711EFE5E48&ID=15050120&M=F).

## Intake hardware reference

Wilkinson specifies a standard 15-inch by 18-inch hand-operated, bottom-hinged intake door. It should read as a modest wall hatch with a handle, not a full-sized doorway. [Wilkinson Hi-Rise, Trash Chutes](https://www.whrise.com/products/trash-chutes/).

CHUTES International's resources likewise identify a standard 15-inch by 18-inch bottom-hinged intake and explain that oversized packaging can jam the chute. Its product description locates the vertical chute inside a shaft, with intake doors on individual floors and discharge below. This supports showing the shaft concealed behind the room wall, with cardboard awaiting separate removal. [CHUTES International, Internal Chutes Resources](https://chutes.com/resource-center/internal-chutes/); [CHUTES International, Trash Chutes](https://chutes.com/products/internal-chutes/trash-chutes/).

## Illustration direction

Composition derived from the references: place the camera inside a small enclosed utility room, with a stainless intake hatch on the rear/left wall and a conventional full-height entry door on the right. Show only a sliver of corridor through that doorway. Continuous room walls and visible corners establish enclosure. Keep apartment doors outside the room. Show bag and cardboard buildup inside this room being loaded onto the owner's requested four-wheel flatbed cart. The cart and pickup activity come from the service brief, not the architectural sources. Avoid an open corridor alcove or a bottom-floor compactor/dumpster room.
